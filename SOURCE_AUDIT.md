# Source and calculation notes

The app uses the seventeen PDFs in `../.docs`. These conventions make the interactive calculations reproducible.

- **Tokens:** Word tokens use Unicode letters and numbers with internal apostrophes. The token lab can case fold, retain punctuation, or use Unicode NFKC. Its BPE demonstration starts from characters inside words, counts adjacent pairs across all occurrences, and omits the end-of-word symbol and deployment refinements of the [Sennrich et al. BPE paper](https://aclanthology.org/P16-1162.pdf). It is labeled as a teaching implementation.

## Lesson 1B expansion conventions

- **Slide count:** `[1b] Words and Tokens.pdf`, pp. 3–9, uses “They grabbed the sandwich that I’m holding, then they ran back to the house and started singing.” With punctuation excluded, clitic attached, and case preserved, the stream is 17 instances / 16 types. Case folding gives 17 / 15; treating comma and period as separate tokens gives 19 / 18 with case preserved. Page 13 changes `started` to `starting` but still prints 17 / 16. The app uses the pp. 3–9 sentence consistently.
- **Clitic:** Splitting `I’m` produces `I` and `’m`; the apostrophe belongs to the clitic, even if other punctuation is excluded. This is an explicit example rule, not a full English contraction tokenizer. Function/content labels apply only to the slide sentence and follow its colored highlights on p. 10, including `I’m` and `then` as highlighted function spans.
- **Normalization:** The equivalence-class mapping and asymmetric/query expansion demos reproduce p. 14 as configurable examples. Accent handling and case folding follow p. 15; the lemmatization example on p. 16 uses a deliberately small disclosed lookup. It does not infer part of speech or headwords for arbitrary input. Unicode NFKC is an extra transform, not an item on the PDF's normalization list.
- **Morphemes:** The root/affix/clitic examples and Chinese segmentation choices come from pp. 17–20. The chosen linguistic analyses are curated; learned BPE pieces beside them use a separate statistical model.
- **WordPiece:** Page 21 says likelihood-oriented merging. The app makes its specific teaching variant explicit: candidate pair score `frequency(pair) / (frequency(left) × frequency(right))`, then longest-match encoding with `##` continuation pieces, following the [Hugging Face WordPiece course](https://huggingface.co/docs/course/en/chapter6/6). This score is illustrative and is not claimed to reproduce the original proprietary WordPiece training procedure or BERT's actual vocabulary.
- **Unigram:** Candidate substrings of up to six characters are initialized from toy-corpus counts. A few forward-backward EM iterations fit piece probabilities. At each step, the app removes the non-character piece causing the smallest immediate change in corpus negative log likelihood, then refits; Viterbi produces the best input segmentation and other high-scoring paths. This is a small teaching model of the probability and pruning ideas in [Kudo (2018)](https://aclanthology.org/P18-1007/) and the [SentencePiece trainer](https://github.com/google/sentencepiece/blob/master/src/unigram_model_trainer.cc), which has further optimization and pruning rules.
- **SentencePiece:** The app shows a `▁` space marker and lets learners run toy BPE or toy Unigram on raw lines. [SentencePiece](https://github.com/google/sentencepiece) implements both algorithms directly on raw sentences and has configurable normalization and whitespace rules. The teaching demo adds a visible start marker and omits production constraints.
- **Byte-level BPE:** UTF-8 bytes are the initial symbols and the learned merges combine frequent adjacent byte groups. The encoder preserves all 256 possible input byte values, including values not seen in the toy corpus. The demo omits GPT-2's pre-tokenizer and printable byte mapping; see [Hugging Face Tokenizers components](https://huggingface.co/docs/tokenizers/components). The comparison table transcribes the five approaches and associated model examples from p. 21 and labels them as examples from the lesson.

## Other lab conventions

- **Edit distance:** Rows represent prefixes of the source; columns represent prefixes of the target. Insertion, deletion, and substitution have editable nonnegative costs; a match costs zero. The default `STRENGTH → TREND` distance is 4 with unit costs and 5 when substitution costs 2, as in `[2a] Edit Distance.pdf`, p. 6.
- **Context vectors:** A context occurrence contributes one count for every neighbor within the selected radius on each side. The target–context matrix is directional. Cosine is undefined for a zero vector.
- **Embeddings:** The trainer uses two dimensional input embeddings, separate output weights, and full softmax. CBOW averages context embeddings; skip-gram predicts each neighbor from the center word. The slide `[2b] Word Embedding.pdf`, p. 15 describes a nonlinear CBOW hidden transformation, while the [original Word2Vec paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1301.3781), §3.1 describes a log-linear CBOW with the nonlinear hidden layer removed. The app explicitly follows and cites the original architecture.
- **N-grams:** The four cat-and-dog training sentences are from `[3a] ngram language modelling.pdf`, pp. 20–24. Each sentence receives start padding and a predicted `</s>` token. `<s>` is not a predicted vocabulary item. Thus `C(the, cat)=3`, `C(the)=8`, and `P(cat | the)=3/8`.
- **Perplexity:** Calculations use unrounded conditional probabilities and include `</s>` in the number of predictions. `[3b] Evaluating Language Models.pdf`, p. 5 rounds intermediate probabilities (`3/8` to `0.37`, for example), so its displayed final value differs from an exact recomputation. Scores are comparable only with identical test text and tokenization. This is consistent with [Jurafsky and Martin, chapter 3](https://web.stanford.edu/~jurafsky/slp3/3.pdf).
- **Smoothing:** Add-one and add-k distributions use the same predicted vocabulary as MLE. The interpolation example uses the weights from `Exercise 3.pdf` and renormalizes over available context orders if a selected history has no counts. Stupid backoff is shown only as a score because it is not a probability distribution, as stated by `[Notes] Interpolation and Backoff.pdf` and [Jurafsky and Martin, chapter 3](https://web.stanford.edu/~jurafsky/slp3/3.pdf). Good–Turing is a separate editable frequency spectrum; ratios needing absent `N_c` values remain undefined.
- **Neural network:** A consistent 2-input, 2-hidden, 2-output demonstration is used. The dimensions on `[4a] Neural Networks for NLP.pdf`, p. 14 appear inconsistent: the shown input example has three values while the shown weight matrix has four input columns. The app does not reproduce those particular numbers.

The course introduction (`[1a] Introduction to NLP.pdf`, pp. 6–9) restricts use of generative AI for submitted assignments. The app includes original prediction prompts and does not provide completed submissions for `exercise1 tokenization.pdf` or `Exercise 3.pdf`.


## Full-course revamp (October 31, 2026)

The course now has 13 lessons and 26 original knowledge checks. The inventory includes the newly supplied `[4d] Masked Language Modeling.pdf`. PDF page references use physical page numbers, including image-only slides. The public site links to primary papers and names the local course sources; it does not redistribute the course PDFs.

- **Orientation:** The telescope example is an original illustration of syntactic attachment ambiguity. The sentiment, entity, translation, and QA examples are curated examples, not outputs of a running language service.
- **Unigram regression:** `conditional` must ignore history when order is 1. JavaScript `slice(-0)` retains the entire array; the former implementation therefore produced incorrect unigram terms when a caller supplied history, including the unigram part of interpolation. It now explicitly uses an empty history. Analytic regression checks cover unigram sequence perplexity and interpolated distribution normalization.
- **Attention:** `[4c] Transformers v2.pdf`, pp. 15–22 and 29–34, and [Vaswani et al., §§3.2–3.5](https://arxiv.org/html/1706.03762v7). The six 2D inputs and two sets of Q/K/V projections are fixed teaching values, not pretrained weights. Scores, row softmax, causal zeros, and weighted outputs are computed from these values. A head selector changes the projection matrices. The visual does not claim to explain learned linguistic relationships. Each displayed head has two dimensions; the lab does not perform the full multi-head concatenation and output projection.
- **Transformer corrections:** The full output projection is `(h × d_v) × d_model`, whereas p. 22 omits the concatenation factor. Causal attention may include its own position and masks strictly future positions before softmax. Original post-layer normalization is distinguished from alternative normalization placements; normalization is not presented as a guarantee against all gradient problems. Sinusoidal positional encoding follows the original formula with `d_model = 8`. Architecture diagrams are schematics, not a running full transformer.
- **Masked language modeling:** `[4d] Masked Language Modeling.pdf`, pp. 7–14, cross-checked against [Devlin et al., §3.1](https://aclanthology.org/N19-1423.pdf). Original BERT selects 15% of token positions and applies an 80/10/10 corruption mixture to the selected set. Our readable 20-word input selects three positions by default; one example of each corruption type deliberately illustrates all cases rather than simulating that mixture. The replacement `tree` is fixed; sliders supply target probabilities. All selected targets, including unchanged tokens, contribute to mean negative-log loss. Nonselected positions still influence representations through attention. Zero selected positions yield an undefined average. The NSP note describes original BERT, without claiming that all encoder models require NSP.
- **Generation:** `[4b] Large Language Models.pdf`, pp. 4–15. The sandbox uses a disclosed, hand-authored last-token logit table and ten output tokens, including an end marker. It is not a pretrained LLM. Temperature rescales logits; top-k masks excluded candidates and normalizes remaining probabilities. Greedy decoding chooses an argmax. Sampling does not update model parameters. The trace stores the probability at the moment the token was generated. Undo restores the preceding context. A 24-token limit prevents unbounded demos.
- **Instruction tuning and retrieval:** [Ouyang et al. (2022)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02155) verifies the supervised demonstration and preference-training discussion. [Lewis et al. (2020)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401) supports the retrieval extension. Neither lower temperature nor retrieval is described as guaranteeing truth.
- **Responsible use:** The supplied `Stochastic Parrots.pdf`, especially pp. 3–10, supports the discussion of costs, documentation, representation, and fluent text. The publisher DOI returned HTTP 403 during verification, so this paper was read from the supplied PDF. The group-accuracy demo uses synthetic values, not real demographic claims. Changing benchmark composition changes the weighted average without changing either group's accuracy. Scenarios are original practice.
- **UI conventions:** Completion means both knowledge-check answers are correct. Answers and the last lesson persist in localStorage only; malformed or unavailable storage does not prevent study. Experiment state is preserved between a lesson's tabs but resets on navigation to another lesson. Embedding autoplay pauses when leaving its experiment tab or unmounting the lesson.

Validation: `pnpm test` covers the existing calculations plus attention normalization, masking, value mixtures, positional encoding, decoding, sampling boundaries, and the unigram regression. DOM integration checks cover all lesson routes, feedback, progress, reload/corrupt storage, tokenization topics, attention controls, BERT loss, generation, search, and paused training. Actual browser screenshot/layout verification was blocked by the environment's socket restrictions; DOM checks do not substitute for a visual browser review.
